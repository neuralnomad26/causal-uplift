Ascarza (2018) showed that focusing only on customers likely to leave may overlook those whose behaviour could actually be changed by an offer [1].

Gutierrez and Gérardy (2017) explain how uplift modelling helps estimate the additional effect of an intervention instead of simply predicting customer responses [2].

Radcliffe (2007) introduced the Qini curve as a way to evaluate how well uplift models identify suitable customers [3].

Künzel et al. (2019) presented the S, T and X learners for estimating treatment effects [4].

Diemert et al. (2018) provided a large advertising dataset that supports research into uplift modelling [5].

Our project builds on these studies by comparing response-based targeting with uplift learners using the Criteo dataset, a fixed holdout set and different training sample sizes.
