Problem: who benefits from an offer, not who is likely to respond, under a limited budget. Hypothesis H1: at the same budget, uplift targeting delivers more incremental outcomes than response targeting. Criteo Uplift v2.1 [6], 13,979,592 users from a randomized advertising experiment, 85 percent treated and 15 percent control, 12 anonymized features, two outcomes, visits and conversions. Metrics: Qini coefficient and extra visits per 100,000 contacted. We keep the 10 most important of 12 features. In a 1M sample of the training pool, the largest standardized difference across the 10 kept features is 0.0536, below the 0.1 threshold. A model predicts treatment slightly better than chance on the holdout (AUC 0.51), so gains may be overstated. The Criteo file is ordered, so we sample at random with a fixed seed.
